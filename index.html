    How the ATS reads your resume

    An ATS parses your resume as plain text, searches for the keywords in the job posting, and scores how well you match. Fancy templates, tables, columns, icons and text boxes often break that parsing — so a clean, single-column resume with standard headings scores higher.

    Our checker simulates that process: it compares your resume against the job description, checks the keywords employers actually search for, and checks the formatting the major US systems — Workday, Greenhouse, Taleo, iCIMS and Lever — need to read your resume correctly.

    Common ATS mistakes

    • Missing keywords: if the job asks for “data analysis” and your resume only says “worked with data”, the ATS may not count it. Use the exact words from the posting.
    • Creative headings: “My Journey” instead of “Experience” confuses the parser. Use Experience, Education, Skills.
    • Tables and columns: two-column designs and tables often scramble the reading order.
    • No dates: every job and degree needs years, e.g. 2022–2025.
    • One resume for every job: tailor the keywords for each posting. It takes 10 minutes and doubles your interview rate for many roles.

    75 or above is strong for most jobs. 50–74 means fix the missing keywords first. Below 50, rewrite the resume around the job description before you apply.
