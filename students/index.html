    How student resumes pass ATS — with little or no work experience

    Campus employers use the same ATS software as big companies — Workday, Taleo, iCIMS and Handshake. They do not expect years of experience from a student. They search for coursework that matches the job, volunteering and club roles that show reliability, and practical skills like cash handling, scheduling and customer service. A one-page resume that names these exactly will outrank a longer resume that hides them.

    Top keywords this checker looks for

    Campus Jobs, Part-Time Work, Coursework, Volunteering, GPA, Student Organizations, Tutoring, Retail Experience, Customer Service, Cash Handling, Time Management, Communication, Teamwork, Microsoft Office, Google Workspace, Social Media, Event Planning, Leadership, Research Assistant, Library Assistant, Food Service, Scheduling.

    Student mistakes to avoid

    • Leaving the resume almost empty: a student resume with only a name and a degree scores badly. Add coursework, clubs, volunteering and any part-time work — even babysitting or food service counts.
    • Listing high school only: once you are at university, lead with your university, degree and expected graduation year. High school belongs at the bottom, or not at all.
    • No GPA when it helps you: if your GPA is 3.5 or above, put it next to your degree. Below 3.5, leave it off and lead with coursework instead.
    • Forgetting campus jobs: library assistant, research assistant, campus ambassador and tutoring are real jobs. Give each one dates and 2–3 bullets with numbers.
    • No numbers anywhere: 'helped at events' scores badly. 'Helped plan 6 campus events for 200+ students' scores well.
    • Using a fancy template: two-column designs and graphics break ATS parsing. Use a clean single-column resume with standard headings.

    Benchmark: the average student resume in our sample scores 48. A score of 75+ puts you well ahead of most applicants for the same campus or part-time posting.

    Frequently asked questions — students

    Can I apply for a campus job with no experience?

    Yes. Campus employers expect students to have little or no work experience. Coursework, volunteering, clubs and part-time work can match most of the keywords in a campus job posting.

    Should I include my GPA?

    Include it if it is 3.5 or above — it is a real keyword for many campus roles. If it is lower, leave it off and lead with relevant coursework and skills instead.

    Do campus jobs count as real experience?

    Yes. Library assistant, research assistant, tutoring and campus ambassador roles all show reliability, communication and time management — the exact traits campus employers search for.
